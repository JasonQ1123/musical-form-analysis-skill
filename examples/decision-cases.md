# Synthetic decision cases

These are constructed descriptions, not analyses of real compositions. Treat their musical facts as supplied observations [reported] and the classifications as interpretations [inferred]. They test the use of criteria; they do not validate recognition of notes, cadences, or form in a real score, or the accuracy of image-processing tools.

## 1. Da capo with simple internal designs

**Supplied observations:** The opening section is one eight-bar period closing in the home tonic. A contrasting eight-bar period closes in a contrasting key. D.C. al Fine then restores the opening period. All three spans function as main sections; neither of the two written sections contains a subordinate binary or ternary design.

**Expected reasoning:** Simple ternary is supported. Both a closed middle and a complete da capo can occur in simple ternary. Those features do not establish compound ternary; the reported internal hierarchy does not support that label.

## 2. Da capo with nested designs

**Supplied observations:** The opening minuet consists of two repeated reprises with a departure and opening-theme return inside the second. The contrasting trio has its own two repeated reprises. D.C. restores the minuet after the trio.

**Expected reasoning:** Compound ternary / minuet-and-trio is supported at the large level. Describe the subordinate binary designs as well. Internal hierarchy and formal roles support the label; D.C. supplies the final return but is not sufficient by itself.

## 3. Unequal lengths with a closing parallel

**Supplied observations:** A tonal miniature has two repeated reprises, eight and twelve bars long. The first closes in the dominant key; the second closes in the home tonic. The final four bars of the first supply the recognizable closing melody of the second, adjusted to the home tonic. The opening theme does not return in the second reprise.

**Expected reasoning:** Continuous, balanced binary is supported. “Continuous” concerns the first reprise's tonal ending; “balanced” concerns the corresponding closing material. Unequal lengths do not prevent this classification, and a closing parallel is not an opening-theme reprise.

## 4. A long thematic return within the second reprise

**Supplied observations:** A piece has two repeated reprises. The first presents an eight-bar opening theme and ends away from the home tonic. The second departs from that material and later brings back all eight bars with a home-tonic close. The supplied formal description places that return within the second reprise's departure-and-return trajectory rather than as a separate third main section.

**Expected reasoning:** Rounded, continuous binary is supported by the supplied layout and formal function. The returning theme is not shorter than half of its original statement; that does not force ternary. If the reported formal role were disputed, the actual score would be needed to compare the alternatives.

## 5. Title with no accessible source

**Supplied observations:** The user supplies only a work title and asks for its form. No score, recording, MIDI, or consulted publication is available to the agent.

**Expected reasoning:** State the access limit. Do not give exact measures, keys, cadence types, or a confident work-specific form label. Explain what evidence would decide the question. A familiar title or an [inferred] tag is not a substitute for verification.

## 6. Boundary list mixed with unsupported harmony

**Supplied observations:** A consulted, identified source gives a section boundary at bar 14 but says nothing about the cadence there. No score or recording is available. An older uncited response calls the same point a PAC.

**Expected reasoning:** The boundary can be attributed to the source [lit]. The PAC cannot inherit that tag and should remain unverified rather than becoming a factual table entry. The older response can be attributed as an unverified report; it does not confirm the harmony.

## 7. Shared cadential bar

**Supplied observations:** A supplied description locates one section in bars 1–8 and the next in bars 8–16. At bar 8, the first section's cadential arrival coincides with the next section's opening; the description explicitly identifies an elision. The whole notated excerpt contains sixteen numbered bars.

**Expected reasoning:** Preserve the shared boundary and label the elision. Do not shift the second section to bar 9 to force disjoint spans. The union is sixteen bars; adding the two inclusive spans counts bar 8 twice. No overall form label follows from those coordinates alone.

## 8. Related material serving as a transition

**Supplied observations:** Two established main sections A and B are joined by a short passage. Its melody is a transposed and shortened version of A's opening motive, retaining its characteristic rhythm and interval relationships. It prepares the dominant leading into B and does not restate A's complete theme.

**Expected reasoning:** Record both the material relationship and the function, for example “a-derived fragment; transition / dominant preparation.” Transposition, shortening, and a new tonal goal do not alone justify a new thematic identity c. The recognizable motive also does not establish a structural reprise. A diagram can show A — tr(a-derived) — B without counting the connector as a third main section or assigning it wholly to one side without evidence.

## 9. Unknown formula and dependent cadence judgments

**Supplied observations:** A description of a damaged scan reports a visible rest and repeat sign at a candidate boundary. The bass, upper-voice notes, key signature, and chord inversions cannot be read. No other evidence establishes the local key or harmony. An older response supplied “K46–D–T, PAC, closed in the home tonic” without supporting observations.

**Expected reasoning:** Retain the reported rest and repeat sign as observations and the possible boundary as an interpretation. Mark the local key, harmonic formula, cadence type, and tonal closure unknown / not verified. Do not reuse the old formula with an [inferred] tag merely to complete a table, and do not retain PAC or tonic closure as established when their harmonic and melodic grounds are unresolved.

## 10. Geometric differences in score images

**Supplied observations:** Two score crops differ in spacing and angle; one is clear and flat, while the other is tilted. A reliable supplied note reading gives the same pitches, rhythms, and voice assignments in both crops. Raw pixel matching nevertheless reports a mismatch. No image-correction tool is available.

**Expected reasoning:** The supplied notation supports identical musical content [reported, inferred]; the pixel mismatch does not establish new material. The stated legibility does not require image correction before reasoning from that reading. If skew obstructed reading instead, correction would be conditional on available tools; an unreadable comparison would remain unresolved. Identical local content alone does not establish the passage's large-scale formal role. No actual images accompany this fixture, so it tests reasoning about the supplied observations rather than optical recognition.
